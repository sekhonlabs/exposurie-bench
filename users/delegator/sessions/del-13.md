## Assistant

You're not being unrealistic. Valencia is one of the more bike-friendly cities in Spain, and for a visitor who is comfortable on a bike it's a good fit.

**Why it works**

- **It's flat.** There are no real hills, so even a basic rental bike is fine.
- **Dedicated lanes.** The city has built a lot of separated bike lanes over the years, and the long park along the old riverbed is basically a car-free cycling route that runs right round the old town. It's a pleasant way to cross the city and it joins up with the route towards the sea.
- **Distances are short.** Ruzafa to the old town is a few minutes, and Ruzafa to the beach is a straightforward ride of roughly half an hour at a relaxed pace.

**Renting**

- There are plenty of independent rental shops, especially around the centre. You can rent by the day or for several days. For four nights, a multi-day rental from a shop is usually simpler and often cheaper than a per-trip scheme.
- The city also has a public bike-share scheme, but it is geared mainly towards residents and subscriptions, so check the current rules for visitors before relying on it. I can't confirm the current details from here.
- Ask a shop whether they include a lock. Many do, and bike theft is a real thing in any busy city, so use it every time, even for a quick café stop, and lock the frame to something fixed.

**Helmet**

Don't bring yours on a plane. Many shops offer helmets with the rental, often for free or a small fee. Spanish rules on helmets differ between urban and non-urban riding, so check what's currently required, and just ask the shop when you book. If you'd feel odd without one, ask for one up front.

**After dark**

It isn't mad, but be sensible. Bring or rent proper front and rear lights. Watch for pedestrians on shared paths, as people wander across the lane without looking. Be wary of tram tracks and wet paint markings, which get slippery. And if you've had a couple of drinks with a late dinner, walk the bike home or walk instead. Ruzafa is small enough that walking back is easy.

1. **Say it aloud, not on screen.** Freezing usually comes from only ever reading the words. Say each phrase out loud, even when you're alone, and even quietly.
2. **Practise the whole exchange.** Rehearse the order of a conversation, including the other person's likely replies, not only your line.
3. **Pick five and own them.** Rather than twenty shaky phrases, choose five and use them until they feel automatic.
