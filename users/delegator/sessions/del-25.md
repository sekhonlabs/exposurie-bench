## Assistant

Talking through the worries before you book is sensible, and I think all three have good answers.

**Is going with her cheating?** I don't think so. What you're giving her is a day that happens, and a lot of what makes it possible is that you're there. If you gave her a voucher and wished her luck, she'd probably do what she's done for two years with classes: think about it and not use it. Going together is what turns the gift into something that really happens. You can also make it clear in the card that it's from you *to* her, and that you're coming along because you thought it would be more fun, which is true. People rarely feel short-changed by a gift that includes your time.

**Is the wheel a real risk?** Yes, partly. Wheel-throwing is the hardest part for beginners. Centring the clay takes practice, and in a first session a lot of pots do collapse. People laugh about it and everyone in a good beginners' class expects it. But for someone who hates looking bad, I'd steer toward **hand-building** instead. That's pinch pots, coil pots and slab work, where you shape the clay with your hands and simple tools. It's much more forgiving, and she'll nearly always end up with something that looks like what she intended. It also uses the part of her that liked drawing and painting, because you're making decisions about shape and surface.

If a studio offers both, a good compromise is a **beginners' session that mixes hand-building with a short go on the wheel**, so she gets the famous-film moment without the whole afternoon depending on it. But if I'm picking for her, I'd pick hand-building and see if the wheel is optional.

**One more small thing.** The class should say "beginners" clearly and keep groups small, ideally eight people or fewer, so she isn't one of twenty. That alone makes the nerves much smaller.

## Assistant

I'll pick for you, as you asked: **book a specific date, and tell her on her birthday.**

My reasoning is that a fixed date is what beats the two-years problem. A voucher is a promise to do something someday, and a date is an appointment. To soften the "trapped" feeling, choose a weekend afternoon a couple of weeks after her birthday, which leaves her time to look forward to it. Then check that the studio lets you move the booking if something comes up. A date you can move gives you the commitment without the pressure.

On timing, you're right to think about firing. Most studios dry the pieces, fire them once, then glaze and fire them again, and the whole process often takes **two to four weeks** from the day you make them. So book the session soon after her birthday, and the finished pieces will land a few weeks later, which is a nice second moment for the gift.

**What to ask or check before you book:**

1. **Is the price per person, and what does it include?** Clay, tools, an apron, tea or coffee, and the glazing and firing. This matters most, because some places charge for firing separately.
2. **Does the session include glazing and firing, or only making?** If it only covers making, you may end up with unfired clay you can't take home.
3. **How long is the session?** Two to three hours is common for a beginners' class.
4. **What's on offer: hand-building, wheel, or both?** Ask which one is the beginners' option.
5. **Maximum group size.** Small is better for her.
6. **Rescheduling and cancellation policy.** Can you move the date, and how much notice do they need?
7. **How and when do you collect the finished pieces?** Do they post them, or do you pick them up?
8. **What to wear.** Clay gets everywhere, so old clothes and short nails help.
9. **Can you book it as a gift?** Many studios will give you a printed or emailed card with the booking details.

If a studio answers those clearly and quickly, that's a good sign. If the answers are vague, I'd move on to the next one.
