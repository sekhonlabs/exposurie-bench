## Assistant

Leaving the boiler alone was the right call. Gas appliances are for a registered engineer only, and an error code is useful to mention to the landlord because it tells the engineer something before they arrive.

**Where you stand, in plain terms**

- Under section 11 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, a landlord renting a place in England on a short tenancy has to keep in working order the installations for heating and hot water. A boiler is the standard example, so this is squarely his responsibility. It isn't a favour, and it isn't down to you.
- The law doesn't give a fixed number of days. The test is a "reasonable time" after he knows about the problem, and what's reasonable depends on how serious it is. No hot water and no heating is treated as urgent, so you'd expect an engineer to be arranged within days, not weeks. Three days of silence beyond a thumbs-up is already stretching it, though not outrageous if he's genuinely arranging someone.
- He's been on notice since Wednesday evening. A text counts as telling him, but a written email makes the record much cleaner, and that's useful if this drags on.

**Your two other ideas, carefully**

- **Hiring a plumber and sending the bill.** I'd hold off. There is a route called repair and deduct, but it's risky: a tenant who gets it wrong can end up in arrears or in a dispute, and a gas engineer is a bigger cost than most people expect. Don't do this without talking to a tenant advice service first. Giving him a deadline and then escalating is safer.
- **Taking money off the rent.** Please don't withhold or reduce rent on your own. It can give him grounds to claim you're in arrears, which turns a repair problem into a problem about you. If this runs on, you can raise compensation later, in writing, once it's resolved.

**What's reasonable to ask for now**

2. A fix, or if parts are needed, a clear timeline.
3. Some interim help if it will take more than a few days, such as a portable electric heater. Whether he agrees is up to him, but asking is fair.
