## Assistant

Yes. The loudest-voice problem is common, and it usually goes away once the process is written down and the same for everyone. For a club of nine, a vote needs only five decisions:

1. **How books get suggested.** Who can put a book forward, how many each, and by when.
2. **When voting closes.** One fixed moment, so nobody argues about whether a late vote counts.
3. **What happens in a tie.** With nine people and a handful of books, ties will happen sooner or later, so decide this now rather than live.
4. **What members see.** Whether they can watch the results as they come in, and what they see after voting.
5. **What the button says.** Small, but it sets the tone of the whole thing.

I'd keep it to a single round: everyone suggests, everyone votes once, the highest total wins. That's easy to explain in one sentence.

Start wherever feels most natural. My guess is suggestions and the deadline, since the rest depends on them.

## Assistant

Your feeling is right, and I can say why. A live count creates a bandwagon. Once someone sees one book ahead, the quieter members, who are already unsure about speaking up, tend to drift toward the leader. Some people vote for the book they think will win rather than the one they want. That's the loudest-voice problem again, only now the loud voice is a bar chart.

So my suggestion is that **results stay hidden until voting closes.** During the week, a member sees the list of books and their own vote, and nothing else. At 20:00 on Sunday the totals appear for everyone at once.

This also helps with honesty. Everyone votes without knowing what the others did, so the result is what the club thinks, not what it thinks it should think. And open and honest still holds: everything is visible in the end, just not early.

One thing to decide, and I'd say yes: when the results do appear, show the full totals for every book, not only the winner. People like seeing how close it was, and it makes the tie rule look fair when it's used.
